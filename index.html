Planning recommendations

Supported by the comparison dataset

  1. For a 2,000–3,500 person indoor event, prioritize Roadrunner or Wang Theatre. Both meet the scale threshold and sit within 500 m of rapid transit.
  2. Use Leader Bank Pavilion for summer events that need 5,000 seats and can support a longer transit walk. Its seasonal setting should remain an explicit weather risk in the event plan.
  3. For 1,000–1,500 seated events, shortlist Jordan Hall or Shubert Theatre before scaling up. A right-sized room avoids paying for excess capacity and can improve audience atmosphere.

Data and method

Reproducible public-source comparison

How we collected it

We recorded each venue’s published maximum capacity, setting, operating season, parking statement, and official address or neighborhood from venue-owner or public-project materials. We used the MBTA’s public GTFS documentation for the transit-data framework. The “transit distance” field is an approximate straight-line distance from venue location to the listed rapid-transit stop, rounded to the nearest 10 meters. Calculations and the complete row-level source links are in the downloadable CSV.

Missing data and limits

Capacities depend on layout, seating, production, and fire-code approvals. The tool does not measure current availability, rental cost, accessibility features, neighborhood impacts, actual walking routes, crowd demographics, ticket demand, or guest satisfaction. Transit distance is not travel time and does not represent service frequency or disruptions. The eight-venue set is selective, so it supports a first shortlist rather than a market-wide conclusion.
