3. Data and methodology

This reading version reproduces the updated one-page note. Download the one-page Word document.

Purpose: help Boston event teams form an initial venue shortlist from the supplied public-source comparison dataset.

Decision question

Which recorded venues cover the audience in the selected setting, season, and configuration? The planner supports attendance from 25 to 7,000 in steps of 25, while disclosing limited small-event coverage.

Dataset and collection

The supplied data/venues.csv contains eight selected venues, seven indoor and one outdoor. Recorded capacities range from 1,019 to 7,500 people. Rows retain venue-owner or public-project source URLs, notes, and the recorded access date 17 September 2026. This update preserves those observations and does not claim a fresh source audit. The sample is not a census. No personal data is collected.

Analysis and recommendations

Capacity must be at least attendance. Setting and configuration are hard filters. Spring, Autumn, and Winter retain year-round venues only; Summer and Not decided retain the seasonal row. Exact operating dates remain unconfirmed. The seated filter includes only the three rows whose notes explicitly say seated. Candidates sort by the smallest sufficient capacity, then name. There is no quality score. Maxima above twice the audience receive a size warning; this is an editorial rule, not a minimum booking size. Revenue equals attendance × $50 before costs, with no demand forecast.

Uncertainty and limits

Capacities mix concert, reception, and seated layouts. Wang Theatre is a candidate for 2,500 seated attendees based on its recorded 3,500. Jordan Hall (1,019) is a candidate for about 1,000, and Shubert (1,500) for 1,500. Pavilion (5,000) is a summer outdoor candidate. Every recommendation requires venue confirmation. The data cannot establish availability, price, accessibility, demand, satisfaction, walking routes, weather, or production feasibility. The sample does not establish suitable small-event rooms. Parking zeros remain unverified.

Sources and reproducibility

Row-level source URLs and dates are in the CSV. Transit values are unverified straight-line estimates: coordinates, stop IDs, and calculation steps were not supplied. GTFS documentation is background only. Boston Landing is commuter rail. Distances are optional filters, not ranking inputs or walking times. build_data.py generates the offline-compatible data file from the original CSV; app.js defines the filters. Run the generator after a CSV edit. The data and all supporting materials are linked on the Project Materials page.

5. Short reflection

What the data supports

VenueFit supports an initial shortlist of the eight venues in the supplied dataset. Their recorded capacities range from 1,019 to 7,500 people; seven are indoor venues and one is an outdoor summer venue. These observations describe the selected sample, not all event spaces in Boston. The dataset does not establish an appropriately sized option for a 25-, 50-, or 100-person event.

What the data cannot prove

Capacities refer to different configurations, including seated theatre, concert, and reception arrangements. They should not be treated as interchangeable. Transit distances are recorded straight-line estimates, not walking routes or journey times. The supplied CSV does not include the coordinates or calculation steps needed to reproduce those estimates. A parking value of zero should not be interpreted as confirmed absence of parking without checking its source.

The data cannot prove which venue is best, cheapest, available, accessible for a particular guest, or likely to achieve better attendance, revenue, or satisfaction. It excludes current quotes, booking availability, service frequency, weather, production requirements, and audience preferences. Candidates sort by the smallest sufficient recorded capacity. This expresses a planning assumption rather than a validated measure of venue quality.

What we learned

Building the planner showed us that a capacity number needs its event layout to be useful. It also showed why an unknown value must be distinguished from a measured zero, and why a ranking rule needs to be explained. Making these limits visible helps users understand when to seek more evidence.

What happens next

The next decision is to contact promising venues, confirm capacity for the intended layout, check accessibility and actual travel routes, and compare availability and quotes. The value of the project is organizing evidence for that conversation while keeping its uncertainties visible.
